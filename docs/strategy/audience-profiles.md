**Document type:** Product strategy and audience research  
**Purpose:** Inform bundle roadmap, onboarding, messaging, pricing and go-to-market sequencing  
**Status:** Working strategy artefact

## 0.0 Executive readout

GreenSquare should not lead with a broad AI productivity promise. The strongest wedge is **decision and document infrastructure for professionals who need to produce defensible work without a trusted advisor beside them**.

The recommended first audience is **ASX200 professionals**, with **Australian SMB operators** as the second strategic expansion path. The most commercially coherent first product remains **The Decision Brief**, because it is specific, demonstrable, repeatable and aligned with the current GreenSquare site positioning.

Important repo alignment note: `CLAUDE.md` currently locks the launch product as **The Decision Brief**, priced at **$49 launch / $59 anchor**, with a later Pro tier around **$129**. This document supports that direction. The higher price points referenced below should be treated as roadmap and elasticity tests, not immediate site changes.

## 1.1 Clear recommendation

**Best initial wedge:** ASX200 professionals, especially managers, senior managers, analysts, transformation leads, strategy, finance, risk and operations professionals.

**Best initial product:** The Decision Brief.

**Best first proof mechanism:** Before and after transformation of messy work context into a concise decision brief with options, assumptions, evidence tags, risks and next steps.

# 2.0 Evidence base and research notes

## 2.1 Known facts

Australian business scale and composition:

- Australia had **2.73 million actively trading businesses** at 30 June 2025.
- Australia had **2.66 million small businesses** and **67,857 medium businesses** by employment size.
- Small businesses represent approximately **97.3%** of Australian businesses.
- The largest business-count categories include construction, professional/scientific/technical services, real estate services, transport/postal/warehousing, health care/social assistance, agriculture, retail, financial/insurance services, administrative/support services, accommodation/food services and manufacturing.

AI adoption:

- ABS reported **12% of Australian businesses used AI in 2024-25**, up from 1% in 2022-23.
- AI use is higher in larger organisations than smaller ones.
- Digital inclusion data indicates higher GenAI use among professionals, managers and students than older age groups.
- National AI Centre reporting indicates many non-adopting SMEs still see AI as not relevant to their business, meaning practical use-case specificity is critical.

## 2.2 Reasonable inferences

- GreenSquare should prioritise users with recurring document, decision and communication pain, not generic curiosity about AI.
- ASX200 professionals are the cleanest first wedge because they combine work urgency, professional ambition, AI readiness and willingness to pay.
- SMB opportunity is commercially larger but requires sharper sub-segmentation because the category is too broad.
- Professional services, health/social assistance and construction/trades are the most attractive SMB starting points because they combine document intensity, decision complexity, admin burden and repeat use.

## 2.3 Hypotheses requiring validation

- Professionals will pay personally for a bundle if a before/after demo makes the quality gap obvious.
- SMB operators will pay more for revenue-linked or compliance-linked bundles than generic operating documents.
- Entrepreneurs will adopt quickly but churn unless the product creates a staged bundle ladder.
- Students will use heavily but present lower price and academic integrity risk.
- Late-career experts may value GreenSquare highly, but onboarding and acquisition friction will be materially higher.

# 4.0 Audience 1, ASX200 professionals

## 4.1 Segment definition

ASX200 professionals are people inside large listed Australian companies who need to make, frame, justify, write and communicate decisions. The strongest fit is analyst to GM-1 level.

| Element | Profile |
|---|---|
| Common roles | Analysts, managers, senior managers, heads of function, project leads, transformation leads, finance, risk, HR, operations, strategy, commercial |
| Work context | Matrixed organisations, executive forums, governance processes, unclear decision rights, high rework risk |
| Likely industries | Banking, insurance, resources, energy, healthcare, retail, infrastructure, telco, industrials |
| Digital maturity | High |
| AI maturity | Medium-high, often unofficial or experimental |
| Buying context | Individual purchase first, team purchase later |
| Buyer type | Individual professional, manager, small team, learning sponsor |

## 4.2 Core job-to-be-done

When I am preparing a recommendation or decision for people more senior than me, I want to structure the logic, options, risks and evidence clearly, so I can produce work that is defensible, concise and credible.

Secondary jobs:

1. Turn messy context into a clean executive brief.
2. Prepare options analysis without weak pros and cons.
3. Pressure-test reasoning before sending work upwards.
4. Adjust detail and tone for a senior audience.
5. Build confidence when working outside my domain.

## 4.3 Decision moments

| Moment | Trigger | Current workaround | GreenSquare intervention | Ideal output | Repeat use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Executive recommendation | GM or ELT asks for a view | Rework old slides, ask a senior colleague | Decision brief intake and options logic | One-page recommendation brief | High |
| Business case | Funding, headcount or system change | Copy prior business case | Business case framework with assumptions | Investment case, risks, benefits | High |
| Options analysis | Competing paths | Pros and cons list | Weighted options analysis | Options table and recommendation | High |
| Governance paper | Committee submission | Internal template plus guesswork | Governance paper builder | Decision paper with evidence tags | Medium-high |
| Risk escalation | Issue needs senior visibility | Long email or fragmented notes | Risk framing and mitigation logic | Escalation note | High |

## 4.4 Pain points

Functional pain:

- Hard to know what good looks like.
- Work is clear in the user’s head but not on the page.
- Internal templates provide structure without decision logic.
- Time pressure forces shallow reasoning.

Emotional pain:

- Anxiety before sending work upwards.
- Fear that a senior stakeholder will expose weak logic.
- Frustration from vague feedback and rework.

Social and professional pain:

- Need to sound executive-ready without pretending.
- Want to be seen as structured, commercial and trusted.
- Poor communication damages perceived capability.

Economic pain:

- Bad briefs delay decisions.
- Rework consumes expensive professional time.
- Weak options analysis can lead to poor capital allocation.

AI-specific pain:

- Generic ChatGPT produces polished but shallow outputs.
- Corporate privacy rules limit what they can paste.
- Users do not trust AI to challenge assumptions properly.

## 4.5 Existing alternatives

| Alternative | Why it is insufficient |
|---|---|
| Generic ChatGPT | Too dependent on the user’s prompting skill |
| Internal templates | Format without thinking logic |
| Google search | Generic, not decision-specific |
| Colleagues | Variable quality and availability |
| Mentors | Scarce and politically constrained |
| Consultants | Too expensive and not available for daily work |
| LinkedIn creators | Useful ideas, not operationalised into work product |
| Blank-page trial and error | Slow and reputation-risky |

## 4.6 Trust barriers and mitigations

| Barrier | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| “This is just prompts” | Dismissal | Show framework, examples, checklist, repeat workflow and privacy guidance |
| Privacy concern | Non-use | Add safe input protocol and redaction guide |
| Fear of weak output | Refund risk | Show before/after demo and quality rubric |
| Corporate compliance | Blocking | Position as user-controlled framework, not a SaaS data capture product |
| Embarrassment | Quiet usage | Anonymous, professional brand with no beginner tone |

## 4.7 Required product capabilities

- Intake fields for decision owner, decision required, audience, deadline, options, constraints, known facts, assumptions and evidence quality.
- Prompt architecture covering advisor role, decision standard, evidence tagging, challenge loop and output format selection.
- Framework documents covering decision framing, options logic, criteria weighting, risk treatment and stakeholder implications.
- Templates for decision brief, options table, executive email, decision log and assumptions register.
- Example outputs showing messy notes to executive brief and weak recommendation to strong recommendation.
- Quality checks such as: would this survive CFO, CRO, COO or GM challenge?
- Evidence tagging using given, inferred, unknown and needs validation.
- Repeat workflow: intake, draft, pressure-test, refine, finalise.

## 4.9 Messaging strategy

Core promise: make better recommendations, faster.

One-line positioning: GreenSquare helps corporate professionals turn messy work context into clear, defensible decision outputs.

Landing page headline: **Make the recommendation before the meeting makes it for you.**

Supporting proof points:

1. Built around decision logic, not generic prompting.
2. Produces executive-ready briefs, options, risks and assumptions.
3. Includes a pressure-test loop before final output.

Pain-led hooks:

- Your problem is not writing. It is unclear decision logic.
- A template will not save a weak recommendation.
- Stop sending long context when the room needs a clear call.
- Before you brief upwards, pressure-test the logic.
- If the decision is messy, structure it before you polish it.

Outcome-led hooks:

- Turn 20 minutes of notes into a one-page decision brief.
- Show the trade-off, not just the preference.
- Make your assumptions visible before someone else does.
- Write like someone who owns the decision.
- Move from update mode to recommendation mode.

Words to use: decision, evidence, assumptions, trade-offs, defensible, concise, pressure-test.

Words to avoid: hack, secret, productivity, magic, prompt pack, AI guru.

## 4.11 Roadmap implications

- MVP features: decision intake, options logic, evidence tags, brief template, pressure-test loop.
- Later features: function variants for finance, risk, strategy, HR and operations.
- Bundle roadmap: Decision Brief, Options Analysis, Business Case, Exec Update.
- Onboarding change: ask “what decision are you trying to make?” rather than “what do you want AI to do?”
- Content requirement: teardown weak corporate writing and rebuild it.
- Trust assets: safe-input guide, example outputs, quality scorecard.
- Pricing implication: keep launch Decision Brief at $49/$59, test Pro pack at $129 later.
- Risk if over-served: becomes a heavy consulting manual.
- Risk if under-served: dismissed as a polished prompt.

## 5.2 Core job-to-be-done

When I know my business but do not have the time or structure to turn that knowledge into strong documents and decisions, I want guided AI support, so I can produce professional outputs without starting from scratch or paying an advisor for every first draft.

Secondary jobs:

1. Create policies, procedures and operating rhythms.
2. Prepare proposals, tenders, client updates and management reports.
3. Improve decision quality and business cadence.
4. Convert owner knowledge into reusable playbooks.
5. Reduce dependence on advisors for first-draft thinking.

## 5.4 Pain points

Functional pain:

- Documentation is inconsistent or missing.

Emotional pain:

Social and professional pain:

Economic pain:

AI-specific pain:

GreenSquare is perceived as a prompt pack rather than a trusted thinking system.

Mitigation: every product page and proof asset should show the system structure: prompt, framework, intake, output schema, examples, quality checklist, repeat workflow and privacy guidance.
