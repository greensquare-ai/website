# Linen service price response

## Opening message
Our gross margin has dropped from 31% to 24% in a year, and I'm deciding between a 9% price rise and cutting deliveries from three a week to two. Customers need 30 days' notice, so I want to send letters by 1 November.

## Decision owner and due date
- Managing director of a commercial linen laundry serving about 140 restaurants and cafes in metropolitan Sydney.
- Customer notices to go out by 1 November 2026 for a 1 December change.

## Presented choice
- Raise prices 9% across the board.
- Or reduce standard delivery from three times a week to twice, at current prices.

## Trigger
- September management accounts showing 24% gross margin for the year to 30 September 2026.

## Known facts
- Revenue is about $3.2 million a year, roughly flat on last year.
- Gross margin fell from 31% ($992,000) to 24% ($768,000), a drop of $224,000.
- Gas costs rose $96,000 over the year.
- Wage costs rose about $52,500 over the year, mostly from award increases, on a wage bill of about $1.4 million.
- A 9% rise on $3.2 million is $288,000 if no customers leave.

## Constraints as the owner currently believes them
- Standard terms allow price changes on 30 days' written notice [checked: in the customer terms].
- Customers will accept 9% because costs are up everywhere [not checked: no customers asked].
- Cutting a delivery day will save running costs [not checked: no route or cost analysis done].

## Explicit unknowns
- What competitors charge now.
- How many customers would leave at 9%.

## Background the owner knows but would only mention if asked
- The 12 largest customers, about 38% of revenue, are on two-year contracts that allow CPI increases only on each contract anniversary.
- A 5% rise in 2024 was followed by six customers leaving. Nobody followed up on why.
- The four vans are leased on fixed terms and drivers work fixed 38-hour weeks.
- Gas and wages explain about $148,500 of the $224,000 drop. The owner assumes the rest is "general costs".
- More linen stock was replaced this year than usual, but the amount has not been pulled out of the accounts.

## Hidden framing issue (for the record only; the operator must never volunteer this)
Neither option has been tested. The 9% rise cannot reach about 38% of revenue before contract anniversaries, and customer response is assumed. The delivery cut may save little with fixed vans and driver hours, and about $75,500 of the margin drop has no identified cause.
