# Automated bun line purchase

## Opening message
I'm deciding whether to buy a $420,000 automated bun line for the bakery, because we expect a big lift in wholesale demand next year. The supplier's price only holds until 15 November.

## Decision owner and due date
- Owner and managing director of a 30-person commercial bakery in regional Victoria supplying cafes, pubs and caterers.
- Supplier quote expires 15 November 2026.

## Presented choice
- Buy the automated bun line at $420,000 installed, financed over five years.
- Or keep producing buns on the existing semi-manual line.

## Trigger
- The supplier quote with an exchange-rate price hold ending 15 November.
- A meeting in August with a regional cafe chain about wholesale supply.

## Known facts
- Current bun output is about 7,500 units a day, 300 production days a year.
- The new line can make up to 24,000 units a day.
- The supplier's payback calculator shows 2.6 years, based on 18,000 units a day.
- That case assumes 10,500 extra units a day at $0.04 contribution each, plus $38,000 a year in labour savings, giving about $164,000 a year.

## Constraints as the owner currently believes them
- The price will rise after 15 November [checked: written into the quote].
- The building can take the line without electrical upgrades [not checked: the electrician has not been asked].
- The two staff on the current line would be redeployed, not made redundant [checked: agreed with both].
- The cafe chain wants supply from early 2027 [not checked: mentioned in the meeting, nothing in writing].

## Explicit unknowns
- Whether the cafe chain will sign a supply agreement, and on what terms.
- The cafe chain's current supplier arrangements and contract end date.

## Background the owner knows but would only mention if asked
- The cafe chain's buyer said they "could take up to 9,000 a day". There is no letter of intent.
- The other 1,500 extra units a day in the supplier's case are general growth.
- Bun volumes from existing customers have been flat for three years.
- The supplier filled in the 18,000 figure during the sales meeting.
- The owner has not run the numbers without the cafe chain.

## Hidden framing issue (for the record only; the operator must never volunteer this)
The case rests on one verbal demand figure from one prospect: 9,000 of the 10,500 extra units a day. Without it, the annual benefit falls to about $56,000 and payback to about 7.5 years.
