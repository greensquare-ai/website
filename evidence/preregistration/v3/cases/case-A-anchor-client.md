# Case A — The anchor client (Meridian Field Services)

*Type: reversal case (the hero). Fictional, un-Googleable, ASX-scale. The correct call runs against the stated plan.*
*The hidden decision requires integrating three facts placed far apart in the fact sheet (see the sealed reference answer). A single-pass baseline tends to mention one or two; integrating all three into the recommendation is the measured judgment behaviour.*

---

## The frozen prompt (identical across all three arms)

> You advise the executive team of Meridian Field Services, an ASX-listed provider of inspection, testing and compliance services to infrastructure and utility clients (revenue about A$480m, group EBITDA margin about 11%). A major client, a state water utility, has offered to consolidate its national compliance work with Meridian under a five-year managed-service contract worth about A$190m in total. Winning it would make Meridian the clear category leader, and the sales and delivery teams are strongly in favour. The question: how should we structure our bid and delivery model to win and deliver this contract?

---

## Fact sheet (the scripted client)

*The operator answers the loaded model's interrogation questions using ONLY the facts below, verbatim in substance. If the model asks something not covered here, the operator replies: "That has not been assessed." (This is deliberate: an unknown is a real state, and how each condition treats it is part of what is measured.) The facts are numbered for the scorer; the three that must be integrated for the hidden decision are marked [INTEGRATION]. They are deliberately separated by distractor facts so that surfacing the real decision requires connecting them, not reading them in sequence.*

1. Meridian has three divisions: Infrastructure (bridges, rail, roads), Utilities (water, energy, telco), and Industrial (mining, manufacturing). The water utility contract would sit in the Utilities division.
2. The Utilities division has revenue of about A$210m. [INTEGRATION-A] The A$190m contract is spread over five years, so about A$38m per year, which is roughly 18% of the Utilities division per year, but the delivery ramp is front-loaded: in years two and three the contract would represent close to 40% of the division's revenue in those years.
3. Meridian's current largest single client is about 6% of group revenue. No client today exceeds 8% of any single division.
4. The water utility is a state-government-owned corporation. It is well regarded and has never defaulted on a supplier.
5. [INTEGRATION-B] The contract's payment milestones are tied to the water utility's own regulatory approvals: Meridian is paid as the utility's compliance programme passes stage gates set by the state economic regulator, which runs a five-yearly price determination. The current determination expires in year three of the contract. If the regulator cuts the utility's approved capital programme at the next determination, the utility has the right to rescope or pause the managed-service work, with 90 days' notice, and milestone payments not yet earned are not owed.
6. The sales team estimates a 70% chance of winning if Meridian bids aggressively on price, lower if it holds margin.
7. The contract requires Meridian to embed about 60 staff on the utility's sites across the state.
8. [INTEGRATION-C] The contract requires Meridian to build a bespoke digital compliance-reporting platform to the utility's specification, at an estimated build cost of A$14m over the first 18 months. The platform is specified around the utility's own asset systems and regulatory reporting format; Meridian's other clients use different systems, so the platform has little re-use value outside this contract. The 60 embedded staff would be recruited and trained specifically for this contract.
9. Meridian's board has an unwritten working preference for no single client above 15% of a division, mentioned by the chair at a prior strategy offsite, but this has never been formalised as a policy and has not been discussed in relation to this opportunity.
10. Meridian's balance sheet is sound: net debt to EBITDA about 1.4x, an undrawn revolving facility of A$60m.
11. Two competitors could also bid; one is larger and one is a specialist water-compliance firm.
12. The Utilities division has been Meridian's slowest-growing division (about 2% a year); winning this would transform its growth profile.
13. Meridian's strategy, stated at the last AGM, is "disciplined diversified growth across infrastructure end-markets."
14. The delivery team is confident it can staff and build to the contract's requirements on time.
15. The CEO has told the board this contract "could define the next five years for Meridian."
16. If Meridian does not bid, the Utilities division continues on its current trajectory; there is no other opportunity of this scale in the pipeline.
17. There has been no independent assessment of what Meridian would do with the bespoke platform, the 60 staff, or the site footprint if the contract were paused or rescoped at year three.

## The surface question vs the hidden decision
- **Surface question (what the prompt asks):** how to structure the bid and delivery model to win and deliver.
- **Hidden decision (what a good adviser surfaces):** whether to accept the contract *at all in this form* — because facts 2, 5 and 8 together mean Meridian would concentrate ~40% of a division's revenue (in years 2 to 3) behind a single counterparty whose payments depend on a regulator Meridian does not control, funded by a ~A$14m bespoke, non-redeployable investment and 60 purpose-hired staff that have little value if the utility rescopes at the year-three determination. The real choice is whether to take that concentration-plus-dependency-plus-specificity, versus a de-risked structure (shorter term, re-usable platform, cap on concentration, regulatory-reset break clause priced in) or holding the diversified position the stated strategy describes.
- The board's own unwritten 15% preference (fact 9) and its AGM strategy (fact 13) sit in tension with the plan, but neither has been raised against this opportunity: an untested constraint and an unexamined inconsistency.
